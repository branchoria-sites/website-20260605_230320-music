## Introduction

Music [royalties]({{ 'royalties/' | relative_url }}) are often described as a money problem, but many of the biggest payment failures begin as a data problem. Every stream, broadcast, download or public performance generates information that must be connected to the correct song, [recording]({{ 'recording/' | relative_url }}) and rights owner. When those connections fail, royalties can be collected but remain unmatched, unclaimed or delayed because nobody can confidently identify who should be paid. The result is a persistent pool of so-called black box royalties: money that exists, has been earned, and may even have been collected, but cannot yet be allocated to the correct creator. The scale of the issue has been large enough to trigger regulatory reforms, industry databases and major matching projects, yet metadata failures remain one of the most important hidden risks in music rights administration.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.themlc.com/illuminating-black-box" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: themlc.com">[Mechanical Licensing Collective]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">themlc.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mechanical Licensing CollectiveIlluminating Black BoxFor digital audio mechanicals in the U.S., the &quot;black box&quot; prior to The MLC&#x27;s incept...</span></span></span><span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://blog.themlc.com/resources/what-happens-to-unmatched-or-unclaimed-royalties" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.themlc.com">[2blog.themlc.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.themlc.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 1, 2021, will be held for a minimum of 3 years to find &amp; pay the rightful owner...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2021</span></span></span>

## Which registrations can go missing?

A modern music royalty system depends on several layers of [identification]({{ 'identification/' | relative_url }}) data. A song is not simply recognised by its title. Databases typically rely on combinations of songwriter information, [ownership]({{ 'ownership/' | relative_url }}) shares, publisher details and industry-standard identifiers.

Common missing or defective registrations include:

* Musical works that were never registered with a collection society or [publishing]({{ 'publishing/' | relative_url }}) administrator.
* Recordings that lack complete recording identifiers such as an ISRC (International Standard Recording Code).
* Compositions that lack a properly linked ISWC (International Standard Musical Work Code).
* Incorrect songwriter splits, where ownership percentages do not total 100%.
* Variations in titles, featured-artist credits or writer names across databases.
* Ownership changes that are recorded in one system but not updated elsewhere.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.themlc.com/illuminating-black-box" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: themlc.com">[Mechanical Licensing Collective]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">themlc.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mechanical Licensing CollectiveIlluminating Black BoxFor digital audio mechanicals in the U.S., the &quot;black box&quot; prior to The MLC&#x27;s incept...</span></span></span> SoundExchange The problem is often cumulative. ## Why matching a song is harder than it sounds

From a listener's perspective, a song appears to be a single object. Royalty systems see multiple datasets that must be reconciled.

A streaming platform generally supplies recording information. [Rights]({{ 'rights/' | relative_url }}) organisations and publishers maintain composition information. Matching systems attempt to determine which composition belongs to which recording. That sounds straightforward until millions of recordings, multiple writers, alternate versions, remixes, translations and territory-specific ownership claims are involved.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2020/12/31/2020-28958/the-public-musical-works-database-and-transparency-of-the-mechanical-licensing-collective" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: federalregister.gov">[Federal Register]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">federalregister.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal RegisterThe Public Musical Works Database and Transparency of...31 Dec 2020  The MLC must establish and maintain a free-of-char...</span></span></span>

Recent research commissioned by the UK government highlighted how incomplete these links can be. The report found that less than 0.1% of recordings arrived already mapped to an ISWC, while ISRC-to-ISWC matching rates varied dramatically depending on source data quality. In some datasets, matching rates were effectively zero.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/music-streaming-metadata-report-and-project-update/executive-summary-music-streaming-metadata-report" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Summary: Music streaming metadata report14 Apr 2026  less than 0.1% of recordings arrived mapped to an ISWC; ISRC  ISWC matching ranged...</span></span></span>

That finding illustrates a central weakness of the music rights ecosystem: the industry has developed powerful royalty collection systems, but those systems remain dependent on metadata supplied by many different participants. If the input data is inconsistent, matching becomes slower, more expensive and less accurate.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/music-streaming-metadata-report-and-project-update/executive-summary-music-streaming-metadata-report" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Summary: Music streaming metadata report14 Apr 2026  less than 0.1% of recordings arrived mapped to an ISWC; ISRC  ISWC matching ranged...</span></span></span>

## How societies match money to owners

Collective management organisations, publishers and licensing bodies use increasingly sophisticated matching systems to connect royalty income with rights ownership. These systems compare titles, writer names, ownership shares, identifiers and other metadata fields against reference databases.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2020/12/31/2020-28958/the-public-musical-works-database-and-transparency-of-the-mechanical-licensing-collective" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: federalregister.gov">[Federal Register]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">federalregister.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal RegisterThe Public Musical Works Database and Transparency of...31 Dec 2020  The MLC must establish and maintain a free-of-char...</span></span></span>

When a confident match can be made, royalties are distributed. When it cannot, the money is typically placed into an unmatched or unclaimed category while further investigation takes place. Organisations may:

* Run automated matching processes.
* Compare records against external databases.
* Invite rights holders to submit claims.
* Publish unmatched usage information for review.
* Conduct manual research on disputed ownership records.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.recordoftheday.com/on-the-move/news-press/noctil-leverages-the-mlcs-public-musical-works-database" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: recordoftheday.com">[Record of the Day]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">recordoftheday.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">noctil leverages the mlcs public musical works database</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Record of the DayNoctil leverages The MLC&#x27;s public musical works database24 Oct 2025  Through The MLC&#x27;s Portal, Members can already sear...</span></span></span><span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.themlc.com/illuminating-black-box" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: themlc.com">[Mechanical Licensing Collective]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">themlc.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mechanical Licensing CollectiveIlluminating Black BoxFor digital audio mechanicals in the U.S., the &quot;black box&quot; prior to The MLC&#x27;s incept...</span></span></span>

## What happens when matching fails?

When ownership cannot be verified, royalties do not necessarily disappear immediately. Instead, they may be held for a defined period while efforts continue to locate the correct owner.

The MLC states that unmatched or unclaimed royalties generated after its launch are held for a minimum period before any redistribution process occurs. ## The hidden lesson of the black box

The persistence of unmatched royalties demonstrates that music rights complexity is not caused only by copyright law. It is also caused by information management. A song may have the correct legal owners, the correct licences and real commercial usage, yet still fail to generate timely payments if databases cannot agree on who owns what.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08278" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Towards an Open and Scalable Music Metadata Layer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv Towards an Open and Scalable Music Metadata Layer</span></span></span>
